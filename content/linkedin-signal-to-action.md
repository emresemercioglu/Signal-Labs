## Variant A: story-led (recommended for the personal profile)

The honest version of what we learned in the last year:

Nobody's competitive intelligence problem was finding the information.

Every team we talked to could already tell us what their competitors had shipped. They'd seen
the launch. They'd read the pricing page. Some of them had a whole Slack channel for it.

What they didn't have was the two hours to update the battlecard, rewrite the comparison page,
and brief the field before the next call. And often they couldn't say what the move actually
meant for an open deal.

So the intel sat there. And the rep on Thursday's call improvised.

That's the gap we spent the year closing. CIx doesn't just track competitors anymore. When one
of them moves, the work shows up already drafted:

→ The competitor page updates itself, and you approve the diff
→ The comparison table, the email to sales, the counter-positioning snippet arrive written
→ Battlecards get flagged with what changed and why
→ Product, marketing and sales work the deal together in a Battle Room, with @CIX in the thread

Collecting intelligence has become close to free. Anyone can point an AI at a competitor's
website. The scarce work is everything after: what the launch means for your roadmap, which
claim to counter on your pricing page, what a rep says when the name comes up mid-deal.

One more thing we didn't compromise on: every claim links to the source it came from. A rep who
can't verify a number won't use it, and they're right not to.

Whether you have a CI team or you *are* the CI team, the point is the same. You should be
editing the work, not producing it.

## Reply bank (for the comments)

**"How is this different from Klue / Crayon?"**
Those are collection and curation tools. They gather intel and leave a human to sort it, write
it up and push it out. We automate that last mile: the page maintains itself, each move comes
with a recommended action, and every claim is source-linked so a rep can check it before
repeating it.

**"Does the AI just publish things on its own?"**
No. Everything sits in an inbox until someone approves it. Auto-approval exists but it's opt-in,
and you scope it to a specific competitor and a specific type of change.

**"We're too small for CI tooling."**
That's most of the people using it. If competitive work currently happens when a founder finds a
spare evening, this is the version of a CI function you get without the headcount.

**"How do you get the data?"**
Public sources only: company websites, pricing and product pages, press releases, news, job
postings, public filings, reviews and plenty more. Every claim links to where it came from.
