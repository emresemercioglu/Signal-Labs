## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does CIx connect competitive moves to market trends?

+

CIx aggregates industry news, analyst reports, regulatory changes, and market signals alongside competitive intelligence. AI-powered correlation analysis links competitor actions (pricing changes, feature launches, market entries) to broader market forces, helping you understand the "why" behind competitive behavior.

### What sources does CIx monitor for market intelligence?

+

CIx monitors industry news sites, analyst reports (Gartner, Forrester), financial data (funding, earnings), regulatory sources, social media sentiment, and competitive intelligence sources. All intelligence is aggregated in one platform, eliminating manual research across disconnected tools.

### How is market intelligence different from competitive intelligence?

+

Competitive Intelligence tells you WHAT competitors do. Market Intelligence tells you WHY they're doing it. CIx provides both in one platform—track competitor actions while understanding the market forces, industry trends, and strategic context driving those actions.

### Who should own market intelligence in our organization?

+

Typically Strategy, Competitive Intelligence, or Product Marketing teams. Market intelligence benefits Leadership (strategic planning), Product (market-driven roadmaps), Marketing (positioning and messaging), and Sales (market context for deals).

### How often is market intelligence updated?

+

CIx monitors market intelligence sources continuously in real-time. Industry news, analyst reports, regulatory changes, and competitive moves are tracked as they happen—no more waiting for quarterly market research reports or manually aggregating intelligence across dozens of sources.

### Can we predict competitive moves with market intelligence?

+

Yes. By analyzing market trends alongside competitive behavior patterns, CIx identifies signals that precede competitor moves. See which market forces correlate with pricing changes, feature launches, or market entries—allowing you to anticipate rather than react.
