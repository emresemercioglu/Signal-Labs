## Two different jobs, honestly described

AlphaSense and CIx are both good at what they were designed for. Here is what each was designed for.

### What AlphaSense is genuinely strong at

AlphaSense is the stronger platform for research depth, and not by a small margin. It combines a very large premium content library with broker research, company filings and financial data, adds expert call transcripts through Tegus, searches your internal documents alongside external sources, and is trusted across investment, banking, consulting and corporate strategy. If your users are analysts who need to understand a market or a company in depth, that is what it was built for.

### What CIx is built for

CIx is not a research platform, and does not try to be. It is a competitive enablement engine: it maintains a living page for each competitor you track, scores every move they make and attaches a recommended action, then generates the battlecards, comparisons and emails your revenue teams actually send. AlphaSense hands an analyst the material to form a view. CIx hands a rep the thing they need before a call.

## Knowing is not the job. Acting is.

Every competitive intelligence tool can tell you something changed. CIx turns that into a specific, previewable change you can ship.

### It revises your intelligence

-   **Update battlecard strengths** Targets the strengths section of the battlecard and drafts the revision, because the change affects how reps should position against them in live deals.
-   **Refresh revenue and ARR** Replaces an estimate on the competitor page with the figure the new filing or announcement actually reported.
-   **Add a strategic bet** Records a product expansion as a new strategic bet, so the picture reflects where they are heading and not only what they shipped.
-   **Add a market trend** Captures a shift in buyer expectations at the category level when a launch signals more than a one-off feature.

### It drafts what goes out

-   **Brief sales on their launch** An internal note with talking points, so reps can respond in deals that are open right now.
-   **Comparison table versus them** A side-by-side built from current intelligence, ready to drop into a deck or a page.
-   **Counter-positioning copy for your site** A ready-to-paste block that answers their new claim in your own words.
-   **Comparison landing page** A full page positioning you head-to-head against their launch, for paid and organic capture.

Every action links back to the signal that triggered it, carries a priority, and shows a preview before anything changes. You approve it, edit it, or discard it — and CIx records who applied it and when.

### Built around the competitor, not the query

CIx organizes everything by the competitors you track and keeps a living page for each, so nobody has to reconstruct a picture from a search session each time.

### Output your revenue team can send

Battlecards, comparison tables, briefing notes and counter-positioning copy come out ready to use, drafted from the signal that prompted them. A research platform gives you the raw material; CIx gives you the finished artifact and applies it on approval.

### Current without a research cycle

Pages update as the market moves rather than when someone next has time to run the analysis, so what your team reads is not last quarter's view.

### Sourced, like research should be

Every claim links back to where it came from. You keep the provenance you expect from a research tool, in a format a seller will actually open.

### Feeds the rest of your stack

A REST API, SDKs and an MCP server let competitive intelligence flow into dashboards, workflows and AI tools instead of staying inside one platform.

## What "automated end to end" actually means

Four steps that normally need a person in the middle. In CIx, the person joins at review.

01

### Gathering happens continuously

CIx tracks each competitor's public footprint — site and pricing changes, product launches, positioning shifts, news, hiring and reviews — and writes the intelligence page itself. There is no research task in anyone's week and no quarterly refresh project.

02

### Every move becomes a scored signal

Changes are not dumped into a feed. Each one is scored for how much it matters, so what reaches your team is a short list of things that actually changed the competitive picture rather than a notification queue to triage.

03

### Each signal turns into a specific action

Not "you should probably update the battlecard" — the action names the section it will change, drafts the new wording, and waits for your approval. Others draft the outbound asset: the note briefing Sales, the comparison table, the counter-positioning copy for your own site.

04

### The output is shaped per team

The same competitor is rendered four ways: objection handling and talk tracks for Sales, roadmap implications for Product, positioning and messaging for Marketing, and trendlines for Leadership. Nobody has to translate one team's asset into another's.

## When AlphaSense is the better choice

We would rather be straight with you than oversell. AlphaSense is the stronger choice if:

-   Your users are analysts who need broker research, company filings or investor-grade market depth.
-   Expert call transcripts and primary interview material are part of how you form a view.
-   You need to search a large internal document corpus alongside premium external content.

These are genuinely different products, and plenty of organizations run both — AlphaSense for the research bench, CIx for the competitive intelligence and enablement your go-to-market teams use week to week.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AlphaSense a competitive intelligence tool?

+

It can be used for competitive research, but that is one use case inside a much broader market and financial research platform. AlphaSense is built primarily for analysts in financial services, consulting and corporate strategy. It is not a competitive enablement product: it does not maintain battlecards or produce seller-facing material.

### What is the difference between AlphaSense and Signal Labs CIx?

+

AlphaSense helps an analyst understand a market or company in depth, using premium documents, expert transcripts and financial data. CIx keeps a living page for each competitor you track, scores their moves with a recommended action, and generates the battlecards, comparisons and emails your revenue teams use. One produces understanding; the other produces enablement.

### Does CIx include expert call transcripts or financial data?

+

No. Expert interview transcripts, broker research and financial data are core AlphaSense strengths and CIx does not attempt to replicate them. CIx works from your competitors' public footprint — sites, pricing and product pages, launches, news, hiring and reviews — plus any documents you upload, with every claim source-linked.

### Can CIx be used alongside AlphaSense?

+

Yes, and that is a common shape. Teams keep AlphaSense for deep research and use CIx as the competitive layer their go-to-market teams work from day to day. CIx also exposes a REST API, SDKs and an MCP server, so its output can flow into the same dashboards and AI tools you use elsewhere.

### Which is better for sales enablement?

+

CIx, without much argument. Sales enablement is what it is built for: battlecards generated from current intelligence, objection handling tailored for reps, and every competitor move accompanied by a recommended action. AlphaSense is a research platform and does not position itself as an enablement product.
