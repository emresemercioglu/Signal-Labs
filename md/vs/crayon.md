## Knowing is not the job. Acting is.

Every competitive intelligence tool can tell you something changed. CIx turns that into a specific, previewable change you can ship.

### It revises your intelligence

-   **Update battlecard strengths** Targets the strengths section of the battlecard and drafts the revision, because the change affects how reps should position against them in live deals.
-   **Refresh revenue and ARR** Replaces an estimate on the competitor page with the figure the new filing or announcement actually reported.
-   **Add a strategic bet** Records a product expansion as a new strategic bet, so the picture reflects where they are heading and not only what they shipped.
-   **Add a market trend** Captures a shift in buyer expectations at the category level when a launch signals more than a one-off feature.

### It drafts what goes out

-   **Brief sales on their launch** An internal note with talking points, so reps can respond in deals that are open right now.
-   **Comparison table versus them** A side-by-side built from current intelligence, ready to drop into a deck or a page.
-   **Counter-positioning copy for your site** A ready-to-paste block that answers their new claim in your own words.
-   **Comparison landing page** A full page positioning you head-to-head against their launch, for paid and organic capture.

Every action links back to the signal that triggered it, carries a priority, and shows a preview before anything changes. You approve it, edit it, or discard it — and CIx records who applied it and when.

## Why teams choose CIx over Crayon

Same category, a different operating model — automated end to end, and tailored per team.

### No curation step

Crayon surfaces candidates for a human to sort. CIx writes the competitor page itself and keeps it current, so nothing sits in a queue waiting to be triaged into usefulness.

### Signals resolve into shipped changes

Every move is scored and turned into a concrete action — revise this battlecard section, send this note, publish this counter-positioning — previewed and applied on your approval rather than added to someone's list.

### Not everything is a battlecard

Sales needs objection handling, Product needs roadmap implications, Marketing needs positioning, Leadership needs the trend. CIx generates all four from the same competitor rather than making battlecards the universal format.

### Enablement without the assembly

Battlecards, comparison tables and competitive emails are generated ready to send. Your product marketers edit for voice instead of building the artefact.

### Verifiable by default

Each claim carries a link to its source, so a rep can check it mid-deal and nobody has to take the intelligence on faith.

### Built for your stack

A REST API, SDKs and an MCP server put competitive intelligence inside your dashboards, workflows and AI tools rather than only inside an enablement asset.

## What "automated end to end" actually means

Four steps that normally need a person in the middle. In CIx, the person joins at review.

01

### Gathering happens continuously

CIx tracks each competitor's public footprint — site and pricing changes, product launches, positioning shifts, news, hiring and reviews — and writes the intelligence page itself. There is no research task in anyone's week and no quarterly refresh project.

02

### Every move becomes a scored signal

Changes are not dumped into a feed. Each one is scored for how much it matters, so what reaches your team is a short list of things that actually changed the competitive picture rather than a notification queue to triage.

03

### Each signal turns into a specific action

Not "you should probably update the battlecard" — the action names the section it will change, drafts the new wording, and waits for your approval. Others draft the outbound asset: the note briefing Sales, the comparison table, the counter-positioning copy for your own site.

04

### The output is shaped per team

The same competitor is rendered four ways: objection handling and talk tracks for Sales, roadmap implications for Product, positioning and messaging for Marketing, and trendlines for Leadership. Nobody has to translate one team's asset into another's.

We would rather be straight with you than oversell. Crayon is the stronger choice if:

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best Crayon alternative?

+

Signal Labs CIx is the closest alternative for teams who want automation rather than a curated program. Crayon is strongest when you have product-marketing capacity to curate intelligence and a mandate to measure adoption and influenced revenue. CIx is strongest when you want competitor pages that maintain themselves, signals that carry a recommended action, and enablement generated per team.

### How is Signal Labs CIx different from Crayon?

+

Crayon is a system your team operates: it monitors broadly, and your product marketers curate what matters and assemble it into battlecards. CIx removes that middle step. It writes and refreshes the competitor page itself, scores each move with a recommended action, and generates the enablement already tailored to Sales, Product, Marketing or Leadership.

### Does CIx replace a competitive enablement program?

+

It removes most of the manual work a program exists to do — monitoring, page upkeep, battlecard authoring and per-team tailoring are automated, so intelligence stays current without a standing process behind it. It does not replace the person. If you have product marketers or a competitive intelligence owner, CIx takes the curation and maintenance off their plate so they can spend the time on positioning, strategy and reviewing output for voice.

### Does CIx report on battlecard adoption or influenced revenue?

+

No. That measurement layer is one of Crayon's real strengths, and if proving program ROI is central to your evaluation, Crayon is the better fit. CIx is focused on making the intelligence current and the enablement ready rather than on reporting how often it was opened.

### Can CIx tailor competitive intelligence for different teams?

+

Yes, and it does so automatically. For every competitor you track, CIx generates distinct recommendations for Sales, Product, Marketing and Leadership from the same underlying intelligence, so each team gets the version relevant to their decisions rather than a single seller-oriented battlecard.
